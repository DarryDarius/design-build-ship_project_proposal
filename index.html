Parallel Days

For graduate students whose plans change mid-day, Parallel Days shows feasible ways forward and makes the cost of each choice visible.

The problem

When a class runs late or a task takes longer than expected, a morning plan no longer fits. The hard part is deciding what to protect and what to postpone.

Who it is for

Graduate students balancing fixed commitments, coursework, longer projects, and personal time.

What potential users told me

“I knew I couldn't finish everything, but I wasn't sure what to sacrifice first.”

Computer science graduate student

“I usually just make decisions in my head. It's hard to see how one delay affects everything else.”

Business graduate student

“When something takes longer than expected, I wish I could quickly compare my options instead of rebuilding my schedule manually.”

Engineering graduate student

The experience

1 · Choose

Enter today's fixed events, priorities, and protected time. Compare feasible plans and choose one.

2 · Replan

When time is lost, record what actually happened. Compare what each new plan preserves or postpones.

3 · Continue

Confirm a revised plan and return to it later. A draft never silently replaces a chosen plan.

Focus: Design

I will go deep on the decision flow: clear comparisons, honest states, accessible timelines, and an understandable account of what changed.

How I will know it works

In a live test, someone can recover from a disrupted plan and explain what their new choice keeps and postpones without my help.

Roadmap

November 10–17 · v1

Interview users. Build real accounts, saved data, manual day input, two feasible plans, and an end-to-end revised choice after lost time.

November 18–23

Watch people use v1. Refine the comparison and the explanation of what each choice changes.

November 25–December 1 · v2

Add a second interruption type and a visual history of the original plan, the disruption, and the chosen response.

Beyond the fair

Explore calendar sync, notifications, and model-assisted planning after the core decision flow works.

Stack and tools

Next.js and TypeScript for the web app and constraint checks; Clerk for sign-in; Supabase for private user data; Vercel for hosting. I will use the Clerk CLI for auth setup, Supabase CLI and MCP for database work, and Vercel CLI for deployment.
